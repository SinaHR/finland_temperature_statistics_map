Methods, data & caveats

What this tool shows

Empirical frequencies of daily temperatures in observational data, and how those frequencies differ between two 30-year reference climates. It is strictly descriptive: it reports what was observed, not why. For attribution and causation questions, see IPCC AR6 WG1 chapter 3 and World Weather Attribution.

Data

FMI ClimGrid daily gridded observational dataset for Finland (~10 km grid, EPSG:3067), 1961–2025, daily mean/maximum/minimum temperature. © Finnish Meteorological Institute, CC BY 4.0, retrieved via the Paituli service (CSC). Reference: Aalto et al. (2016), doi:10.1002/2015JD024651. Exact file checksums are pinned in the processing repository.

Windows and estimation

  • A query pools all days within ±15 days of the chosen calendar date (a 31-day window) across the 30 years ending in the chosen reference year, inclusive: reference year R covers R−29…R. The baseline is fixed at R = 2000, i.e. 1971–2000. Windows wrap across the year boundary (early-January windows include late-December days of the preceding years). Feb 29 is dropped. Sample size ≈ 31 × 30 = 930 daily values per estimate; for R = 2025, windows near the end of December contain slightly fewer (≥ 915) because January 2026 does not exist yet.
  • Daily values are pooled into fixed 0.5 °C bins spanning −45…+40 °C; the handful of Lapland minimum temperatures below −45 °C count in the lowest bin. Thresholds snap to 0.5 °C. “At least X” is the observed share of days with T ≥ X; “at most X” the share with T < X + 0.5 °C (bin resolution).
  • Dates snap to the nearest of 52 precomputed weekly window centers (at most 4 days away; the ±15-day pooling makes neighboring windows nearly identical).
  • The frequency ratio divides the chosen window’s observed share by the baseline share. Both absolute shares are always displayed with it: a ratio alone can mislead when both shares are tiny.
  • If a window contains no days at/beyond the threshold, the tool reports “not observed (fewer than ~1 in 930 days)” and shows no ratio — nothing is extrapolated or smoothed.

Caveats

  • Consecutive days are strongly autocorrelated, so the ~930 pooled values contain far fewer independent samples. Point estimates are unbiased, but no uncertainty bands are shown, naïve intervals would be misleadingly narrow.
  • The 31-day window blends the seasonal trend within the window.
  • Colloquially, “chance of a 30 °C day” refers to the daily maximum variable.
  • The two 30-year windows overlap for reference years before 2030; differences between overlapping windows understate differences between disjoint periods.
